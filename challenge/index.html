The challenge

One loop. Real users.

In four weeks, launch something real and useful that people use every week, and let its routine run without you. A newsletter that writes itself every morning. A student radio that never sleeps. An internship scout. A leaderboard that updates itself. The topic is open. The users must be real.

What good looks like

Good targets for the final demo

A guide, not a rule. Aim high: real use beats a perfect pitch.

Targets
  • 30 or more real users outside your team, or 3 paying customers.
  • At least two weeks of running on its own, every day, with a log of every run.
  • People come back: show use week by week.
  • Your minutes per day, and the cost per good result.
  • Stretch: the first euro from a customer, a sponsor or an advertiser.

Be honest about it

What counts as “runs without you”

A planned human approval can be the right safety limit. Hidden manual work is not autonomy.

Does not count
  • You copy each request into a chat by hand.
  • You send each result yourself.
  • You press “run” for every order.
  • It only writes drafts, but you call it a full service.
  • Test users or a recorded demo shown as live use.
Fine, if you say so
  • A human approves risky steps. Name the step and count the minutes.
  • You invite your first users by hand.
  • Your first version runs by hand. Call it a manual test.
  • You test with made-up data first, labelled as made up.

Your plan

Four weeks to the final demo

One plan. Real users from week 1. Something better every week.

  1. This week
    Design and choose

    Write your six lines with the exercise prompt. Talk to five real users: how do they solve it today, and would they use your thing every week? On Thu 15 Oct, form your team and choose this challenge.

    No idea yet? Seven ideas from the lecture →

  2. Week 1
    Practise and ship

    Every day, build tiny things with an AI coding agent:

    1. A form that emails you a clean summary of each answer.
    2. A morning digest: read a sheet, send the three most important rows.
    3. A question maker with a checker that rejects any question the text cannot answer.

    By Sunday, a first version in front of five real users. By hand is fine: call it a manual test.

  3. Week 2
    The loop runs

    One request in, one checked result out, no clicks from you. It runs every day. 10 real users. Five steps to a running loop →

  4. Week 3
    Grow it

    Failures handled, a control panel, 30 real users. Try for the first euro from a customer, a sponsor or an advertiser.

  5. Week 4
    Learn and prove

    One change from real feedback, old and new tested on the same examples. Your numbers week by week. Rehearse the 10-minute demo.

  6. End of the course project
    Final demo

    Show the six things on a service that really runs. SSE sets the date.

Every week: five short lines on what works, what users got, what broke, how often you stepped in, and what you test next. Every day: 30 minutes on what is new.

Questions

FAQ

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You need to learn to direct AI agents and to check their work. If you don't know something, ask AI, then check what it says. Start with tiny things in week 1.

We have no idea yet. What now?

Start from a real problem that you, or people near you, have every week. Or take one of the seven ideas from the lecture and change it for a user you can reach.

Does it have to make money?

No. A useful free service or an internal tool with a real user counts. If it is paid, show how orders and payments fit the loop. Do not invent revenue.

Who is a real user?

A person outside your team who has the problem and uses your service. For an internal tool, one named user inside an organisation is fine.

Can a human still approve things?

Yes. A planned approval for a risky step can be the right limit. Say where it is and count your minutes. The routine work should run without you.

Which tools must we use?

Any. n8n is a good start, and Dmitrijs shows it at 14:00 on 13 October. You can also build with Codex or Claude Code, or use another tool that runs your loop on an event or a schedule.

Where do we get help?

Ask AI first, with the prompts on the lesson page. For running your loop, start with the n8n quickstart and the workshop. Questions about the challenge: danielius.stasiulis@gmail.com.

What about data and safety?

Test with made-up or permitted data first. Without a human approval, your system must not spend money, make payments, send mass messages or change real customer data. Tell users that AI makes the result, and what data you keep. Never share one personal account password inside a team.

When is the final demo?

At the end of the course project. SSE sets the date.

Is this a recruiting event?

No. The goal is to learn to build. Working together later is a bonus, not a condition.
